# The Well to Hell — Kola Superdeep Borehole

## Facts and sources
- Drilling began 24 May 1970, Kola Peninsula, Pechengsky district, near the Norway border. Target 15 km. — Wikipedia "Kola Superdeep Borehole"
- Hole diameter 23 cm (9 in). — same
- Passed 12,000 m in 1983; deepest point 12,262 m in 1989. Deepest ocean point (Challenger Deep) ~10,935 m. — same; Wikipedia "Challenger Deep"
- Microscopic plankton fossils (24 species) found ~6 km down. — same
- Bottom temperature 180 °C (expected 100 °C); rock behaved plastically, drilling stopped 1992. — same
- "Well to Hell" hoax: aired by Trinity Broadcasting Network 1989; audio traced to the "Baron Blood" (1972) soundtrack. — Wikipedia "Well to Hell hoax"
- Site abandoned 2008; borehole capped with a welded metal cap. — Wikipedia, photos of the cap

## Voice-over (approved 2026-11-22)
In 1989, a rumour said Soviet scientists drilled so deep, they heard people screaming.
The Arctic, near Norway. The USSR wants the deepest hole on Earth. Target: fifteen kilometres. Width: about a dinner plate.
By 1983, they pass twelve kilometres. Deeper than the deepest ocean.
At six, they pull up fossils. Tiny sea plankton, buried for millions of years.
Then a Christian TV network says they lowered a microphone, and recorded the damned. Hell, apparently, is in Murmansk.
Wait. Then why did they really stop?
You see, down there it's a hundred and eighty degrees. Rock stops acting like rock. It's like drilling into hot plasticine. Pull the drill out, and the hole squeezes shut.
And the screams? Lifted from a 1972 horror movie soundtrack.
Today, the deepest hole humans ever made is covered with a rusty, welded lid.
So. Did they quit because of the heat, or because of what they heard?
